# Cutoff Eight: the Certified Order-at-Most-81 Slice

## Scope

`[COMPUTED]` The canonical certificate
`experiments/logs/h8_bounded_cutoff.json` solves every central-extension
graph in the already certified 738-type inventory with

\[
|Q|=|G/Z(G)|\le 81
\]

at clique cutoff eight.  Its conclusion is deliberately bounded: among this
inventory the maximum abelian-cover number remains ten, and no example with
\(a>10\) occurs.

`[UNVERIFIED]` This is not a global upper bound for \(h(8)\).  The cutoff-seven
argument used the verified subgroup-cover theorem \(f(7)=81\); no corresponding
global reduction to \(|G/Z(G)|\le81\) is asserted for \(\nu(G)\le8\).

## Ordinary batches

`[PROVED]` If two vertices of a simple graph have equal open neighbourhoods,
then they are nonadjacent independent twins.  Collapsing each such class to
one representative preserves both clique and chromatic number: a clique uses
at most one member of each class, while a coloring of the quotient lifts by
giving every member of a class its representative's color.  Conversely,
restricting any coloring or clique to representatives gives the opposite
inequality.

`[COMPUTED]` The five cutoff-seven ordinary batches contain 14,989 faithful
rows that had been pruned by a saved eight-clique.  They give 12,266 distinct
stored adjacency tuples.  Independent-twin compression reduces every one to
at most 56 vertices.  Exact maximum-clique calculations leave exactly four
rows with clique number eight:

| \(Q\) | SmallGroup | kernel serial | \((\omega,\chi)\) |
|:---|:---:|---:|:---:|
| \(D_{14}\) | \((14,1)\) | 1 | \((8,8)\) |
| \(C_7:C_3\) | \((21,1)\) | 1 | \((8,8)\) |
| \(C_7:C_6\) | \((42,1)\) | 1 | \((8,8)\) |
| \(C_7\times C_7\) | \((49,2)\) | 1 | \((8,8)\) |

Each record stores an exact eight-clique, a verified eight-coloring, and the
eight commuting color classes.  Every other one of the 12,262 distinct
adjacencies has a saved verified nine-clique.  The full exact-\(\omega\)
census, both by distinct adjacency and with row multiplicity, is in the JSON.

`[PROVED]` The saved commuting color classes are abelian-cover generators,
not merely set colors.  Representatives within one class commute pairwise in
the corresponding central extension.  Therefore the subgroup generated by
their lifts is abelian; adjoining the center gives an abelian subgroup of
\(G\).  The eight generated subgroups cover all central cosets because the
color classes cover the quotient vertices.

## Eleven generic order-64 dual searches

`[PROVED]` The target-nine BFS is complete within each exported character
module.  First, any character whose scalar graph has a nine-clique excludes
every character subgroup containing it.  Starting with the trivial subgroup,
the search adjoins every remaining scalar character and then takes its full
conjugation-invariant closure.  If a child has a nine-clique, every supergroup
retains that clique and may be pruned.  Conversely, every invariant subgroup
with no nine-clique admits a generating chain all of whose invariant closures
remain inside it, so the BFS reaches it.

`[PROVED]` If elements of \(Q=G/Z(G)\) do not commute, then arbitrary lifts
do not commute in \(G\): commuting lifts would have commuting images.
Consequently \(\nu(G)\ge\nu(Q)\).  The exact 29- and 10-cliques therefore
exclude SmallGroups \((96,227)\) and \((144,196)\) before any extension-graph
scan.

`[COMPUTED]` In all three chosen covers, the image of the cover center in
\(Q\) has size one, so this selected-cover check supplies no nonidentity
universal zero-row exclusion.  This is only complementary feasibility data;
no converse capability assertion is made.

`[PROVED]` The abstract quotient \(C_3^2\times A_4\) has noncommuting clique
number five.  The abelian direct factor only creates independent twins.  In
\(A_4\), a clique contains at most one of the three commuting double
transpositions and at most one element from each of the four Sylow-3
subgroups.  Choosing one double transposition and one generator from each
Sylow-3 subgroup attains five.

### [PROVED] Exact extensions of \(C_3^2\times A_4\) cross cutoff nine

Let

\[
\pi:G\longrightarrow Q=C_3^2\times A_4
\]

be an exact central quotient, meaning that \(\ker\pi=Z(G)\).  Then
\(\nu(G)\geq9\).  Thus none of the 84 normal exterior kernels in the
feasibility row for \(\operatorname{SmallGroup}(108,41)\) can give a
cutoff-eight candidate; the following argument does not depend on that
kernel enumeration.

Write \(A=C_3^2\times1\leq Z(Q)\), additively, and use the commutator
convention \([x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy\).  For \(a\in A\) and \(q\in Q\), choose
lifts \(\widetilde a,\widetilde q\in G\) and put

\[
b(a,q)=[\widetilde a,\widetilde q].
\]

Because \(A\leq Z(Q)\), this commutator belongs to \(Z(G)\).  Multiplying
either lift by an element of \(Z(G)\) does not change it, so \(b\) is
well-defined.  The usual commutator identities become multiplicative in
each variable because all these commutators are central.  Moreover
\(b(a,q)^3=b(3a,q)=1\).  Hence the subgroup \(W\leq Z(G)\) generated by
the values of \(b\) is an elementary abelian 3-group, and \(b\) may be
viewed as an \(\mathbf F_3\)-bilinear map \(A\times Q_{\mathrm{ab}}\to W\).

The pairing has zero radical in its \(A\)-variable:

\[
b(a,q)=1\quad\text{for every }q\in Q\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad a=0.
\tag{H8.1}
\]

Indeed, under the hypothesis any lift \(\widetilde a\) commutes with a lift
of every element of \(Q\), and it also commutes with the kernel \(Z(G)\).
Thus \(\widetilde a\in Z(G)\), so its image \(a\) in the exact quotient is
zero.

Put

\[
\beta(a,a')=b(a,a')\qquad(a,a'\in A).
\]

This is an alternating \(\mathbf F_3\)-bilinear map.  Choose a 3-cycle
\(t\in A_4\) whose image generates
\(A_4^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong C_3\), and put \(T(a)=b(a,t)\).  If
\(\epsilon(h)\in\mathbf F_3\) denotes the image of \(h\in A_4\) in this
abelianization, then

\[
b(a,h)=\epsilon(h)T(a)
\tag{H8.2}
\]

in additive notation on \(W\).  For two points in the same \(h\)-fiber,
the commutator identities give the explicit formula

\[
[\widetilde a\widetilde h,\widetilde {a'}\widetilde h]
 =\beta(a,a')+b(a,h)-b(a',h)
 =\beta(a,a')+\epsilon(h)\bigl(T(a)-T(a')\bigr).
\tag{H8.3}
\]

Changing a displayed product lift by a central kernel element has no effect
on the formula.

There are two cases.

1. If \(\beta=0\), (H8.1) and (H8.2) say that \(T:A\to W\) is injective.
   In the \(t\)-fiber, (H8.3) is \(T(a-a')\), which is nonzero whenever
   \(a\ne a'\).  All nine points
   \(\{(a,t):a\in A\}\) therefore lift to a noncommuting set.

2. Suppose that \(\beta\ne0\).  Since
   \(\bigwedge^2 A\) is one-dimensional, \(\beta(a,a')\ne0\) whenever
   \(a,a'\) are linearly independent.  Choose one representative from
   each of the four one-dimensional subspaces of \(A\), and choose a
   nonidentity double transposition \(v\in A_4'=V_4\).  Equation (H8.3),
   with \(\epsilon(v)=0\), shows that the four corresponding points in the
   \(v\)-fiber form a clique.

   The graph in the \(t\)-fiber has an edge.  Otherwise (H8.3) would vanish
   for every distinct \(a,a'\).  Taking \(a'=0\) first gives \(T(a)=0\)
   for every nonzero \(a\), hence \(T=0\); taking linearly independent
   \(a,a'\) would then give \(\beta(a,a')=0\), a contradiction.

   Now take \(t\) and one 3-cycle from each of the other three Sylow-3
   subgroups of \(A_4\).  Together with \(v\), these five elements form a
   noncommuting set in \(A_4\).  Use the four-point clique in the
   \(v\)-fiber, a two-point edge in the \(t\)-fiber, and one arbitrary
   point in each of the other three fibers.  Points in different fibers
   are noncommuting already in the quotient \(A_4\), while (H8.3) handles
   points within a fiber.  The resulting \(4+2+1+1+1=9\) lifts are
   pairwise noncommuting.

Both cases prove \(\nu(G)\geq9\).
