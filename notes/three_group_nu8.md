# Finite \(3\)-groups at cutoff eight

This note treats only finite \(3\)-groups. Its main result is that the
clique-eight branch cannot increase the abelian-cover number.

## The theorem

### [PROVED] The clique-eight branch has an eight-member abelian cover

Let \(P\) be a finite \(3\)-group. If

\[
\nu(P)=8,
\]

then

\[
a(P)=8.
\tag{TG.1}
\]

The proof is split into several lemmas. We use the conventions

\[
g^u=u^{-1}gu,
\qquad [g,u]=g^{-1}u^{-1}gu,
\]

and write

\[
\delta_u(k)=k^{-1}k^u.
\]

### [PROVED] Two members of the clique-centralizer cover are maximal

Choose a maximum noncommuting set
\(X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_8\}\), and put

\[
H_i=C_P(x_i).
\]

The eight distinct proper subgroups \(H_i\) form an irredundant cover of
\(P\). Indeed, an element outside their union would extend \(X\), while
\(x_i\) belongs to \(H_i\) and to no \(H_j\) with \(j\ne i\).

At least one \(H_i\) is maximal: otherwise every \(H_i\) has index at least
nine and their union has size at most \(8|P|/9<|P|\). Relabel so that
\(H_1\) is maximal. It is normal of index three. For \(i>1\), the element
\(x_i\) lies in \(H_i\setminus H_1\), so \(H_1H_i=P\) and

\[
[H_i:H_i\cap H_1]=3,
\qquad
|H_i\setminus H_1|=\frac23|H_i|.
\tag{TG.2}
\]

If all seven \(H_i\), \(i>1\), were nonmaximal, then

\[
|P\setminus H_1|
 \le \sum_{i=2}^8|H_i\setminus H_1|
 \le 7\frac{2|P|}{27}
 <\frac{2|P|}{3}=|P\setminus H_1|,
\]

a contradiction. Thus at least two of the \(H_i\) are maximal.

### [PROVED] Maximal-centralizer amplification

Let \(x\in P\) and suppose that \(H=C_P(x)\) is maximal. Let \(y\notin H\),
and let \(K\le Z(H)\) be a nontrivial \(y\)-invariant subgroup on which
\(y\) acts nontrivially. Set

\[
q=|\operatorname {im}(\delta_y:K\longrightarrow K)|.
\]

Then

\[
\nu(H)+q\le8.
\tag{TG.3}
\]

To prove this, first note that \(H\triangleleft P\). The map
\(\delta_y\) is a homomorphism because \(K\) is abelian. If \(T\) is a
transversal of \(\ker\delta_y\) in \(K\), direct multiplication gives

\[
(yt)(yu)=y^2t^yu,
\qquad
(yu)(yt)=y^2u^yt.
\]

Consequently

\[
yt\text{ commutes with }yu
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\delta_y(t)=\delta_y(u),
\tag{TG.4}
\]

and \(\{yt:t\in T\}\) is a \(q\)-clique.

Let \(\{h_1,\ldots,h_r\}\) be a maximum clique in \(H\), where
\(r=\nu(H)\). Since \(K\le Z(H)\), for \(k,t\in K\) we have

\[
(h_jk)^{yt}=h_j^yk^y.
\]

It follows that

\[
h_jk\text{ commutes with }yt
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(h_j^{-1}h_j^y)\delta_y(k)=1,
\tag{TG.5}
\]

independently of \(t\). For a fixed \(h_j\), at most one value in
\(\operatorname {im}\delta_y\) makes the right side equal to one. Since
\(q\) is a nontrivial power of three, \(q\ge3\), and we may choose \(k_j\)
whose image avoids that value. Centrality of \(K\) in \(H\) preserves all
edges among the \(h_jk_j\). Therefore

\[
\{h_1k_1,\ldots,h_rk_r\}\ \cup\ \{yt:t\in T\}
\]

is an \((r+q)\)-clique, proving (TG.3).

There is always a suitable \(K\). Since \(x\in Z(H)\), \(Z(H)\) is
characteristic in the normal subgroup \(H\), and \(y^3\in H\), the subgroup

\[
K_0=\langle x,x^y,x^{y^2}\rangle\le Z(H)
\]

is \(y\)-invariant. Moreover, \(\delta_y(x)\ne1\), because
\(y\notin C_P(x)\). Thus (TG.3) applies with \(K_0\).

### [CITED-VERIFIED] The small finite-\(3\)-group inputs

Berkovich, *Coverings of finite groups by few proper subgroups*,
*Glasnik Mat.* 45 (2010), has finite standing scope on printed p. 415.
Lemma 1.2 on p. 416 gives \(\nu(R)\ge p+1\) for a nonabelian finite
\(p\)-group \(R\). Theorem 4.4 on pp. 424--425, applied to the irredundant
centralizer cover of a maximum clique, excludes \(\nu(R)=p+2\) when \(p\)
is odd. Theorem 2.3 on pp. 419--420 describes the equality case
\(\nu(R)=p+1\) as

\[
R=MZ(R),
\tag{TG.6}
\]

where \(M\) is minimal nonabelian (and \(M\cap Z(R)=Z(M)\)). These exact
pages and hypotheses are already recorded in audit/source_audit.md.

### [PROVED] Consequences for a nonabelian maximal centralizer

If \(H=C_P(x)\) is maximal and nonabelian, then

\[
\nu(H)=4.
\tag{TG.7}
\]

Indeed, maximal-centralizer amplification gives \(\nu(H)\le5\). The
preceding verified inputs give \(\nu(H)\ge4\) and exclude five.

In this case

\[
H/Z(H)\cong\mathbf F_3^2,
\qquad |H'|=3,
\qquad H'\le Z(H).
\tag{TG.8}
\]

Here is a self-contained derivation from (TG.6). Choose noncommuting
\(a,b\in M\). Minimal nonabelianness gives \(M=\langle a,b\rangle\), and
every maximal subgroup of \(M\) is abelian. For every \(g\in M\), the
cyclic subgroup \(\langle g\rangle\) is proper and lies in a maximal
subgroup together with \(\Phi(M)\). Hence \(g\) centralizes \(\Phi(M)\), so
\(\Phi(M)\le Z(M)\). The Frattini quotient is generated by two elements,
and \(M/Z(M)\) cannot be cyclic; consequently

\[
M/Z(M)\cong\mathbf F_3^2.
\]

Since \(M'\le\Phi(M)\le Z(M)\), the group \(M\) has class two. Its derived
group is generated by \([a,b]\), and
\(a^3\in\Phi(M)\le Z(M)\) gives
\([a,b]^3=[a^3,b]=1\). Nonabelianness now gives \(|M'|=3\). Finally,
\(H=MZ(H)\) and \(M\cap Z(H)=Z(M)\), so (TG.8) follows.

### [PROVED] A nonabelian outer intersection forces scalar symplectic
geometry, hence is impossible at clique number eight

Keep \(H=H_1=C_P(x_1)\) maximal, and for \(i>1\) put

\[
L_i=H\cap H_i=C_H(x_i).
\]

We claim that every \(L_i\) is abelian. Suppose, to the contrary, that
\(L=C_H(y)\) is nonabelian for \(y=x_i\).

Then \(H\) is nonabelian, so (TG.7)--(TG.8) apply. The image of \(L\) in
the two-dimensional symplectic space \(H/Z(H)\) cannot have dimension at
most one: every one-dimensional subspace is isotropic, which would make
\(L\) abelian. Therefore

\[
LZ(H)=H.
\tag{TG.9}
\]

Put \(Z=Z(H)\). Since \(y\) centralizes \(L\), (TG.9) gives

\[
[H,y]=[Z,y]=J,
\qquad
J=\operatorname {im}(\delta_y:Z\longrightarrow Z).
\tag{TG.10}
\]

The image is nontrivial: \(x_1\in Z\), whereas \(y\notin C_P(x_1)\).
Apply maximal-centralizer amplification with \(K=Z\). Since
\(\nu(H)=4\), it gives \(4+|J|\le8\). The order of \(J\) is a nontrivial
power of three, and hence

\[
|J|=3.
\tag{TG.11}
\]

Both \(H'\) and \(J\) are central in \(P\). For \(H'\), conjugation by
\(y\) has order dividing three because \(y^3\in H\), while
\(\operatorname {Aut}(H')\cong C_2\); hence \(y\) centralizes \(H'\).
For \(J\), conjugation by \(y\) again has order dividing three on \(Z\),
the subgroup \(J=\operatorname {im}(\delta_y)\) is \(y\)-invariant, and
\(\operatorname {Aut}(J)\cong C_2\). The subgroup \(H\) centralizes both
because both lie in \(Z(H)\).

We next prove that

\[
J=H'.
\tag{TG.12}
\]

If not, these are distinct central subgroups of order three and have trivial
intersection. Since \(L\) is a nonabelian subgroup of \(H\),
\(\nu(L)=4\); choose a four-clique \(R\subseteq L\). Choose a transversal
\(T\) of \(\ker\delta_y\) in \(Z\), so that

\[
F=\{yt:t\in T\}
\]

is a three-clique by (TG.4). More precisely, for \(t,u\in T\), direct
multiplication gives

\[
[yt,yu]=\delta_y(t)\delta_y(u)^{-1}\in J,
\tag{TG.13}
\]

which is nonidentity when \(t\ne u\). Every member of \(R\) commutes with
every member of \(F\): elements of \(R\subseteq L\) commute with \(y\), and
elements of \(H\) commute with \(Z\).

Consider the twelve actual group elements

\[
\mathcal C=\{rf:r\in R,\ f\in F\}.
\tag{TG.14}
\]

For \(r,s\in R\) and \(f,g\in F\), all cross commutators are trivial and
the two remaining commutators are central, so

\[
[rf,sg]=[r,s][f,g].
\tag{TG.15}
\]

If only \(r\ne s\), the first factor is a nonidentity element of \(H'\); if
only \(f\ne g\), the second is a nonidentity element of \(J\); and if both
coordinates differ, their product cannot be one because
\(H'\cap J=1\). Thus distinct index pairs in (TG.14) give distinct,
pairwise noncommuting elements. Equivalently, injectivity may be checked
directly: an equality \(rf=sg\) would give
\(r^{-1}s=fg^{-1}\in Z(H)\), which makes \(r\) and \(s\) commute and hence
forces \(r=s\), followed by \(f=g\). This is a twelve-clique, contradicting
\(\nu(P)=8\), and proves (TG.12).

Equations (TG.10) and (TG.12) now give

\[
P'=\langle H',[H,y]\rangle=H'.
\]

The group \(P=\langle H,y\rangle\) therefore has class at most two and
derived group of order three. In particular \(g^3\in Z(P)\) for every
\(g\in P\), because
\([g^3,h]=[g,h]^3=1\). Hence

\[
V=P/Z(P)
\]

is an elementary abelian \(3\)-group, and commutation defines a
nondegenerate alternating scalar form

\[
b:V\times V\longrightarrow H'\cong\mathbf F_3.
\tag{TG.16}
\]

Its radical is zero by the definition of \(Z(P)\), so
\(\dim_{\mathbf F_3}V=2m\). The centralizer \(H=C_P(x_1)\) is nonabelian,
which excludes \(m=1\): in a symplectic plane, the perpendicular space of
a nonzero vector is one-dimensional and its full preimage is abelian.
Commutation in \(P\) depends only on (TG.16), and all nondegenerate
alternating forms of the same dimension over \(\mathbf F_3\) are isometric,
so Corollary SS.2 of notes/scalar_symplectic_family.md applies. If \(m=2\),
the proved scalar-symplectic value \(\pi(3,2)=7\) gives \(\nu(P)=7\).
If \(m\ge3\), Proposition SS.4 in the same note gives
\(\nu(P)\ge3m+1\ge10\). Both alternatives contradict \(\nu(P)=8\).
This proves that every \(L_i\) is abelian.

### [PROVED] The eight abelian subgroups

Define the private cell of \(H_1\) by

\[
Q_1=H_1\mathbin{\big\backslash}\bigcup_{i=2}^8H_i,
\qquad A_1=\langle Q_1\rangle.
\]

The set \(Q_1\) is pairwise commuting: two noncommuting members of \(Q_1\),
together with \(x_2,\ldots,x_8\), would form a nine-clique. Therefore
\(A_1\) is abelian.

For \(i>1\), the normal subgroup \(H_1\) has index three and
\(x_i\in H_i\setminus H_1\). Thus

\[
H_i=\langle x_i,L_i\rangle.
\]

The subgroup \(L_i\) is abelian by the preceding lemma, and \(x_i\)
centralizes it by definition. Consequently every \(H_i\), \(i>1\), is
abelian. Finally,

\[
P=A_1\cup H_2\cup\cdots\cup H_8.
\tag{TG.17}
\]

Indeed, outside \(H_1\) this follows from the original centralizer cover;
inside \(H_1\), an element either lies in some
\(L_i=H_1\cap H_i\) or lies in \(Q_1\subseteq A_1\). Thus \(a(P)\le8\).
Every abelian cover has at least one member for each element of an
eight-clique, so \(a(P)\ge8\), proving (TG.1).

## Cutoff consequence

### [PROVED] Exact finite-\(3\)-group contribution at cutoff eight

Among finite \(3\)-groups with \(\nu(P)\le8\), the largest possible
abelian-cover number is ten.

For \(\nu=8\), the theorem above gives \(a=8\). The already proved exact
global values \(h(6)=6\) and \(h(7)=10\) bound the branches
\(\nu=6\) and \(\nu=7\), respectively; the lower bound \(a\ge\nu\) makes
\(a=6\) in the former branch. The verified finite-\(3\)-group results above
give \((\nu,a)=(4,4)\) at \(\nu=4\) and exclude \(\nu=5\). Finally, the
scalar symplectic group \(S(3,2)\) has

\[
(\nu,a)=(7,10)
\]

by Theorem SS.3 and the proved value \(\pi(3,2)=7\) in
notes/scalar_symplectic_family.md. Hence the finite-\(3\)-group maximum at
this cutoff is attained and equals ten. This conclusion does not classify
all finite \(3\)-groups in the \(\nu=6\) or \(\nu=7\) branches, and it is
not a determination of the unrestricted value \(h(8)\).
